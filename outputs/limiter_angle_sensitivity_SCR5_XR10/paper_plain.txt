Uniﬁed Model of Current-Limiting Grid-Forming
Inverters for Large-Signal Analysis
Nathan Baeckeland , Member , IEEE,B o w e nY a n g, Member , IEEE, and Gab-Su Seo , Senior Member , IEEE
Abstract—Grid-forming (GFM) inverters can hardly withstand
any overloading. As such, GFM inverters need a current limiter in
their control system to avoid hardware damage during overloading
or grid disturbances. When the current limiter is engaged for
inverter protection, however, it dynamically changes the inverter
output impedance, which can signiﬁcantly affect the system be-
havior against large disturbances. For example, when the current
limiter curtails the output power, the primary controller of the
GFM inverter—which generates the voltage and angle reference—
is prone to losing synchronization with the grid. This can cause
large-signal instability in the network. This paper presents a uniﬁed
GFM current-limiter model to gain a deeper understanding of
the impact of the GFM inverter current limiting on large-signal
instability and other system behaviors. The uniﬁed model captures
several well-known current limiters for GFM inverters, and it
reveals their large-signal equivalence characterized by the lim-
iter angle. We use the uniﬁed model to present in-depth insights
into the driving forces that cause large-signal instability in GFM
inverter-driven grids. Further, this paper illustrates that the uniﬁed
model can be used as a tool for assessing the system-level impacts
of various GFM current-limiter designs during grid disturbances.
Using numerical, analytical, electromagnetic transient models, and
a hardware setup, the validity of the uniﬁed model is evaluated.
Index Terms —Grid-forming inverter current limiting, large-
signal stability, equivalent-circuit model, power-angle curve,
virtual impedance, power system transient stability.
I. I NTRODUCTION
GRID-FORMING (GFM) inverter research and development
has gained signiﬁcant traction in recent years. Unlike their
contemporary grid-following (GFL) counterpart, GFM inverters
create their own voltage and frequency reference, enabling grids
to operate at high levels of inverter-based resources (IBRs).
For these reasons, adopting GFM capabilities in grid-connected
inverters has been urgently called for by system operators,
research institutions, and companies around the world [1], [2],

proves challenging, and many research questions are yet to be
answered [7], [8]. Especially determining how GFM inverters
should behave during grid disturbances (such as (un)balanced

electronics inverters, such as semiconductor switches, have
limited overloading capabilities that barely exceed the nomi-

optimization [12]. To that end, a current limiter is an essential
block in the inverter control system which strictly limits the
output current to not cross hardware limitations that can cause
irreversible inverter damage. Although the existential purpose
of the limiter is solely to prevent the inverter output currents
from exceeding the acceptable value, the limiter design pro-
foundly inﬂuences the entire dynamic behavior of the inverter
during current-limited conditions. Namely, because the current
limiter dynamically alters the output impedance of the inverter,
network-wide attributes for a power system, such as transient
stability, power system protection, and voltage support, can be
compromised. The device-level and system-level impacts of the
GFM inverter current limiter are visually represented in Fig. 1,
and it illustrates the need to investigate the GFM current limiting
to understand system resilience during large disturbances in an
IBR-dominated grid [13].

current limiting on transient stability are illustrated through
simulations and hardware experiments. These works assume
idealized scenarios without ﬁlter or line resistance or assume a
current limiter where the current-phasor angle is ﬁxed. In [19],
the authors present an equivalent circuit model to compile the
power-angle characteristic for a GFM inverter with a circular
current-reference limiter and validate the theoretical results

for virtual impedance (VI)-based current limiters. The authors
illustrate that inductive VI limiters inherently induce better
stability margins compared to resistive VI limiters, which we
further acknowledge in this work. In an attempt to guarantee
GFM inverter transient stability, despite the stringent current
limits, other works have proposed additional control blocks to
the primary controller, which—directly or indirectly—modify
the perceived output power of the inverter and with it, the power-
angle dynamics [21], [22], [23], [24], [25], [26]. A comparative
overview of these methods is given in [27].
To better map and understand the inﬂuence of GFM current
limiting on large-signal stability, this paper presents a uniﬁed
model framework for GFM current-limiting methodologies. In
that way, this work has a similar approach to [28], where the
authors present a uniﬁed equivalent-circuit model that captures
circular current limiting and virtual impedance limiting. In addi-
tion to the work in [28], this paper presents deeper understand-
ings into the driving mechanisms behind large-signal instability
with GFM inverters, based on novel theoretical derivations, and
provides an in-depth sensitivity analysis of various attributes
that induce instability. Initiated from the uniﬁed current-limiting
framework, we aim to provide answers to the following ques-
tions: How can we intuitively understand the power-angle char-
acteristics of GFM inverters under various types of current
limiting? How do various current-limiting methods affect GFM
inverter transient stability, both on a device level and system
level? How can we derive an analytical model that captures
various current-limiting methods to understand the power-angle
dynamics of GFM inverters and derive critical clearing angles
and times? With these questions in mind, this paper presents the
following contributions:
C1 Proposing a uniﬁed GFM inverter current limiter model
that captures the large-signal behavior of GFM inverters
with inner control loop-based current limiters, including
the current-reference saturation limiter (also known as
the circular current limiter or magnitude limiter) with
anti-windup feedback, and the VI limiter. This gener-
alized framework can be used to perform theoretical
analyses of grid-connected GFM inverters under various
current-limiting scenarios, such as large-signal stability
analysis.
C2 Deriving a numerical and analytical model for the power-
angle characteristics of GFM inverters under current
limitations. This provides a deeper understanding of the
signiﬁcant impact of current limiting on the GFM inverter
stability during grid disturbances and the inﬂuence of
control parameters and grid conditions.
C3 Presenting full-order electromagnetic transient (EMT)
simulations—both of a single-inverter and a realistic
multi-inverter network simulation—along with a hard-
ware setup, to support the theoretical ﬁndings on the
large-signal stability of GFM inverters under current-
limited conditions.
The rest of this paper is structured as follows. In Section II,
we begin by introducing pertinent notations used throughout
the paper. In Section III, we introduce the generic GFM inverter
control architecture which will serve as a baseline throughout
this work, and propose the concept of the uniﬁed current limiter
framework. Following, in Section IV, we set up a numerical
and analytical model to characterize the current-limited GFM
inverter while encompassing all current limiter variations within
the uniﬁed framework. Based on the analytical model, we pro-
vide an in-depth analysis of GFM inverter power-angle charac-
teristics under current limiting and examine limiter dynamics
under various grid conditions. In Section V, we dive deeper
into the mechanism driving GFM inverter large-signal instabil-
ity under various current-limiting strategies. We visualize and
validate the numerical and analytical results through full-order
EMT simulations—both in a single-inverter and multi-inverter
network simulation—and a hardware setup of various grid dis-
turbances. Finally, Section VI concludes the paper.

IV . THEORETICAL ANAL YSIS OFUNIFIED MODEL AND
LARGE-SIGNAL ST ABILITY
Following the conceptual introduction of the uniﬁed frame-
work presented in Section III, this section establishes theoretical
models to provide deeper insights into how various current lim-
iter types impact the transient-stability characteristics of GFM
inverters and how these dynamics interact with different grid
conditions. In this section, we ﬁrst refresh the classic concepts
of large-signal stability analysis rooted in traditional power
systems theory, as we continue to use these well-established
methods to analyze GFM-inverter stability boundaries. Then,
we present a numerical model of the uniﬁed framework as
a benchmark, followed by an analytical model for in-depth
analysis.
A. Large-Signal Stability With Synchronous Generators
In its most simpliﬁed form, an SG can be represented as
a voltage source, E, behind an impedance, ZSG. Assuming a
purely inductive internal impedance, the SG active output power
can be expressed as a function of the rotor angle using the
well-known power-angle equation [31]:

internal rotor angle of the SG follows the droop equations in (1),

rather than calling upon the individual limiter types, and we
denote them by ‘Limϕlim ’. For example, the limiter Lim 0 could
be the SatLim with ϕvi =0 ◦, or the VI-Lim with ϕvi =0 ◦, and
both will have nearly identical p(δ′) characteristics. Remark:
Although the p(δ′) curves for SatLim and VI-Lim are nearly
identical, one must be mindful that the p(δ′) curves only cap-
ture quasi-static behaviors, and do not capture the small-signal
response of current limiters.
This numerical model sets the benchmark to compute p(δ′)
curves which can be leveraged to assess the large-signal stability
of GFM inverters; however, it might not provide insights into
how these unconventional curves are formulated or what drives
the behavior of such characteristics. The following derivation of
the analytical model provides an answer to these questions.

numerical and analytical power-angle characteristics of current-

inductive current limiter increases the overall inductiveness of

margins of the inverter, ii) increases active power support during
current limiting under angle-related conditions, and iii) improves
grid-voltage support during voltage drops.

V. L ARGE-SIGNAL ST ABILITYANAL YSIS OFGFM INVERTERS
AND IBR-DOMINANT POWER SYSTEMS
Leveraging the derived p(δ′) characteristics, we can analyze
the large-signal stability (i.e., transient stability) of GFM invert-
ers and inverter-dominated power systems in a similar manner
as with SGs. Analyzing and mapping the transient-stability
margins of GFM inverters provides insight into how robust a
GFM-dominated power system is during transients and distur-
bances. Similar to classical transient-stability approaches, we
start from a single inverter connected to an inﬁnite bus setup
and expand to investigating the stability of a multi-generator
inverter-driven power grid.
We ﬁrst consider a generic grid voltage drop to 0.5 pu and
conceptually explain the differences in the power-angle dynam-
ics of GFM inverters when employing either a Lim0 (i.e., purely
resistive) or Lim 90 (i.e., purely inductive) limiter. (Though in-
ertia response in the primary controller is not considered, the
following outlined principles remain the same.) Through simu-
lations, we then assess the GFM transient stability under three
generic fault types: voltage sag, phase jump, and frequency shift.
Notice that these fault types, or a combination of those, constitute
all realistic faults that can occur in a grid (e.g., a line fault is
a combination of a voltage drop, phase shift, and potentially
followed by a frequency swing). Following, we validate the
device-level results through a hardware experiment. Lastly, we
present simulation results of a multi-inverter 14-bus network
that is subjected to realistic unbalanced line faults and illustrate
system-level transient stability and unbalanced voltage support
characteristics of the interconnected GFM inverters using the
proposed uniﬁed model.

E. Large-Signal Analysis of Power Systems Using
Multi-Inverter Network Simulations
Leading up to this point, we analyzed large-signal stability for
one GFM inverter connected to an inﬁnite bus. In this section,
we illustrate, through simulations, that general trends derived in
the theoretical analysis of this paper carry over to network-wide
fault conditions. We also illustrate and discuss the impact of the
GFM inverter current-limiter settings on other network-wide
attributes, including system stability and system-wide voltage
support. The goal of this network-wide simulation effort is
threefold. First, we aim to showcase the validity of the uniﬁed

VI. C ONCLUSION
The current limiter in a GFM inverter is an essential part of the
control system to protect the inverter against overcurrents caused
by system disturbances. As a side effect of curtailing the output
current during contingencies, the current limiter dynamically
alters the output impedance of the GFM inverter. This has signif-
icant implications for network-wide attributes and systems, such
as network-wide stability, inverter synchronization with the grid,
power system protection, and dynamic network voltage support.
To deepen the understanding of GFM inverter current limiting
and its effects on large-signal stability, this paper presented
a uniﬁed current-limiter model. The model incorporates two
common current-limiting methods into one continuous alge-
braic model, which enables various theoretical analyses, such
as large- and small-signal stability analysis and model order
reduction. From the uniﬁed model and building on classical
power system stability concepts, we derived an analytical ex-
pression for the power-angle characteristics of GFM invert-
ers under various current-limiting conditions. This theoretical
framework elucidates the mechanisms of large-signal stability
in GFM inverters and the role of current limiting in exacerbating
instability in a refreshing and succinct manner. We validated the
proposed uniﬁed model and analytical model-based power-angle
characteristics using numerical computation, full-order EMT
simulations for various fault scenarios, and hardware exper-
iments. The system-level stability and network-wide voltage
support studies illustrated how the proposed uniﬁed current-
limiter model framework can serve as a tool for system-level
analyses. The multifaceted modeling and analysis conﬁrm the
validity of the uniﬁed models of current-limiting GFM inverters
for device-level and system-level large-signal studies. Notably,
using the uniﬁed model, this work revealed the equivalence of
inner-control-based current-limiting methods. Regardless of the
limiter type, e.g., SatLim or VI-Lim, their large-signal behavior
can be characterized by their impedance angle. Future work
includes extending the uniﬁed current-limiter model to more
current-limiter types and exploring the applications of the model
for large power system studies in greater detail.
